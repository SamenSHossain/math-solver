**Complete proof (no `sorry`, standard axioms only), following Lemma 2.2 of the manuscript (an adaptation of Seacrest's Lemma 4).**

Setting: V finite, r an oriented graph, N1(v) = firstNeighbors r v, N2(v) = secondNeighbors r v, F = fwd r. hbad gives |N2(v)| < |N1(v)| for every v. All helper lemmas live in namespace OAI.SeymourSecondNeighborhood.Sol_OAI_SeymourSecondNeighborhood_lemma_2_2; `theorem solution` is top-level and just pairs `posIndeg` and `subsetDeficit`.

Preliminaries. mem_firstNeighbors / mem_secondNeighbors / mem_fwd unfold the classical filters (simp only with Finset.mem_filter, mem_univ). fwd_union (F(A ∪ B) = F A ∪ F B), fwd_mono, fwd_empty (F ∅ = ∅).

(0) good_of_restrict: for W : Finset V, u ∈ W with every out-neighbour of u in W, goodness of ⟨u,hu⟩ in the induced subgraph (fun a b : W => r a b) implies goodness of u in r. Proof: the image under Subtype.val of the restricted first neighbourhood equals N1(u) (uses the hypothesis for ⊇), and the image of the restricted second neighbourhood is contained in N2(u) (paths in W are paths in V); Subtype.val is injective, so Finset.card_image_of_injective and Finset.card_le_card give |N1 u| = |N1' u| ≤ |N2' u| ≤ |N2 u|.
good_of_sub_eq: if r' ⊆ r and r v w ↔ r' v w for all w (v loses no out-arc), then GoodVertex r' v → GoodVertex r v, since N1_r' v = N1_r v and N2_r' v ⊆ N2_r v.

(1) posIndeg: suppose v has no in-neighbour. If univ.erase v is nonempty, it is a nonempty proper subset, hmin_order yields a vertex u good in the induced subgraph; every out-neighbour w of u satisfies w ≠ v (else r u v), so w ∈ univ.erase v, and (0) makes u good in r, contradicting hbad. Otherwise every vertex equals v, so N1(v) = ∅ by looplessness and v is good (0 ≤ _), contradicting hbad.

(2) subsetDeficit. Fix S nonempty proper; write E := F S \ S, I := F S ∩ S and g(T) := |F T \ (I ∪ T)|.
 step (core lemma): for T ⊆ E, T ≠ E, there is a nonempty Q ⊆ E \ T with g(T ∪ Q) + 1 ≤ g(T) + |Q|. Let D := E \ T (nonempty) and del r S D a b := r a b ∧ ¬(a ∈ S ∧ b ∈ D). This is a subrelation of r missing at least one arc (any w ∈ D has an in-neighbour x ∈ S), so hmin_arcs gives v good for del r S D. By good_of_sub_eq and hbad, v ∈ S and Q := N1(v) ∩ D is nonempty. Facts proved: (F1) N1_del(v) = N1(v) \ Q; (4) if del v u and del u w then w ∈ I ∪ T ∪ F T; N1(v) ⊆ I ∪ T ∪ Q. With C := F Q \ (I ∪ T ∪ Q ∪ F T): (C1) C ⊆ N2(v) (via q ∈ Q with r v q, r q w; w ≠ v by asymmetry; ¬ r v w since N1(v) ⊆ I ∪ T ∪ Q); (C2) C is disjoint from N2_del(v) by (4); (G) N2_del(v) ⊆ (N2(v) \ C) ∪ (Q ∩ F T) (a vertex of N2_del(v) not in N2(v) must satisfy r v w, hence w ∈ Q, and lies in F T by (4) since Q ∩ (I ∪ T) = ∅). Counting with Finset.card_sdiff_add_card_eq_card and card_union_le, plus goodness of v in del and |N2 v| < |N1 v|, omega yields |C| + 1 ≤ |Q| + |Q ∩ F T|. Finally the exact identity F(T ∪ Q) \ (I ∪ (T ∪ Q)) = ((F T \ (I ∪ T)) \ Q) ∪ C (disjoint union, via fwd_union and tauto) together with (F T \ (I ∪ T)) ∩ Q = Q ∩ F T and card_sdiff_add_card_inter gives g(T ∪ Q) + |Q ∩ F T| = g(T) + |C|, hence g(T ∪ Q) + 1 ≤ g(T) + |Q|.
 induct: by strong induction on n = |E \ T|: for all T ⊆ E, T ≠ E, g(E) + 1 ≤ g(T) + |E \ T|. Take Q from step; if T ∪ Q = E conclude using |Q| ≤ |E \ T|; otherwise |E \ (T ∪ Q)| + |Q| = |E \ T| with |Q| ≥ 1, apply the IH to T ∪ Q and add.
 Conclusion: E ≠ ∅ (else F S ⊆ S and hmin_order on S plus (0) produces a good vertex of r). Applying induct with T = ∅ and simplifying (F ∅ = ∅, g(∅) = 0) gives g(E) + 1 ≤ |E|. Finally F(F S) \ F S = F E \ (I ∪ E) because F S = I ∪ E and F I ⊆ F S (fwd_mono), so |F(F S) \ F S| = g(E) < |E| = |F S \ S|, which is SubsetDeficit r.
