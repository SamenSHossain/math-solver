**Proof sketch of Seymour's second-neighborhood conjecture, following the manuscript *A proof of Seymour's second-neighborhood conjecture* (OpenAI, September 23, 2026).** The main theorem is reduced to three platform theorems, all stated over the definition block `SeymourSecondNeighborhood_Image` and all Open: `OAI.SeymourSecondNeighborhood.lemma_2_2` (subset deficit of a minimal counterexample), `OAI.SeymourSecondNeighborhood.lemma_2_3` (transitive-tournament blow-up) and `OAI.SeymourSecondNeighborhood.prop_4_1` (no positive-indegree oriented graph is a strict expander). The reduction itself is complete and uses no `sorry`.

**Argument.** We prove, by strong induction on $n=|V|$, that every oriented graph on a nonempty finite vertex set has a good vertex. Suppose the oriented graph $(V,r)$ with $|V|=n$ has none. Among the subrelations $r'\subseteq r$ in which every vertex is bad (there is one, $r$ itself), choose $r_0$ with the minimum number of arcs. Then:
- $r_0$ is oriented, and every vertex is bad for $r_0$;
- every proper subrelation of $r_0$ has a good vertex (it has fewer arcs, so by minimality it is not all-bad);
- every nonempty proper induced subgraph $r_0[W]$ has a good vertex, by the induction hypothesis applied to the subtype $W$ (which has fewer than $n$ vertices).

These are exactly the hypotheses of Lemma 2.2, which yields that every vertex of $r_0$ has an in-neighbor and that $r_0$ has the subset deficit $|F^2(S)\setminus F(S)|<|F(S)\setminus S|$ for every nonempty proper $S$. Lemma 2.3 with $m=n+1>|V|$ then shows that the blow-up $r_0[T_{n+1}]$ on $V\times\{0,\dots,n\}$ is an oriented graph with positive indegrees satisfying $|U|+|F^2(U)|<2|F(U)|$ for every nonempty proper $U$. Proposition 4.1 says no such graph exists. This contradiction proves the induction step, and the theorem follows with $n=|V|$.

**Formalization notes.** The arc count is `(univ.filter (fun p : V × V => r' p.1 p.2)).card` with classical decidability, and the minimal all-bad subrelation is obtained with `Nat.find`; a proper subrelation has strictly smaller arc count (`Finset.card_lt_card`). The induced-subgraph hypothesis of Lemma 2.2 is stated for the subtype `↥W` with the restricted relation `fun a b : W => r a b`, and is discharged from the induction hypothesis using `Fintype.card W = W.card < Fintype.card V`. The `Nonempty (V × Fin (n+1))` instance needed by Proposition 4.1 comes from `Classical.arbitrary V` and the label `0`.
